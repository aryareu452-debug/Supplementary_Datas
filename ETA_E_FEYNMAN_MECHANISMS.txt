I. IN-GAP DEFLECTION MECHANISMS (0 < Location < d)

1. Lepton–Lepton In-Gap Interaction (e⁻ ──γ*──> e⁻) [Møller Scattering]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: Electromagnetic Coulomb repulsion between an incoming 
thermionic electron and another electron in the space-charge cloud via a 
virtual photon (γ*).
Kinetic Outcome: Inverts or deflects forward momentum vector, repelling the 
electron back toward the emitter or out of bounds, reducing eta_e.

Feynman Diagram:

  e⁻ (incoming) \               / e⁻ (scattered / deflected)
                 \  γ* (virtual) \
                  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
                 /               \
  e⁻ (in-gap)   /                 \ e⁻ (repelled space-charge e⁻)


2. Lepton–Boson In-Gap Interaction (e⁻ ──∅──> γ) [Compton / Thomson Scattering]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: Direct kinetic scattering between an incoming thermionic 
electron and a real photon (γ) traversing the radiative thermal flux gap.
Kinetic Outcome: Shifts the electron momentum vector pe beyond the geometric 
acceptance window, preventing it from contacting the collector.

Feynman Diagram:

  e⁻ (incoming) \               / e⁻ (scattered)
                 \  e⁻ (virtual) \
                  \               /
                   \_____________ /
                   /             \
  γ (real photon) ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ γ (scattered photon)

II. COLLECTOR SURFACE NON-ABSORPTION (Location = d)

3. Lepton–Quark Interaction: Backscattering (e⁻ ──γ*──> q) [Flies Away]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: The incoming electron exchanges a virtual photon (γ*) with a 
single bound valence quark (q) inside a target nucleon (proton/neutron) within 
a collector nucleus and retains enough energy to scatter back into the gap.
Kinetic Outcome: Electron backscatters back into the vacuum gap, failing to deliver 
usable current J_th * V_ext and reducing eta_e.

Feynman Diagram:

  e⁻ (incident) \               / e⁻ (backscattered / flies away)
                 \  γ* (virtual) \
                  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
                 /               \
  q (bound)     /                 \ q' (recoiled bound quark in nucleus)


4. Lepton–Quark Interaction: Kinetic Energy Transfer (e⁻ ──γ*──> q) [Gets Stuck]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: Electromagnetic coupling to nuclear bound quarks (q) converts 
the electron's kinetic energy (p_e^2 / 2m_e) into nuclear recoil and lattice 
vibrations (phonons/TC).
Kinetic Outcome: Continuous deceleration against nuclear potential fields causes 
the electron to lose escape momentum and drop into a bound/conduction state.

Feynman Diagram (Microscopic Multi-Vertex Cascade View):

  e⁻ (incident) ───●───────────●───────────●───═══ e⁻ (stuck / bound in lattice)
                   │           │           │
                   │ γ*        │ γ*        │ γ*
                   │           │           │
                 q_1         q_2         q_3   (bound quarks in nuclei)
                   │           │           │
                   ▼           ▼           ▼
              [Lattice Vibration / Phonon Generation → Waste Heat TC]


5. Lepton–Lepton Interaction: Secondary Ejection (e⁻ ──γ*──> e⁻) [Flies Away]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: The incoming electron strikes a target electron in the 
collector via γ*, transferring enough momentum to eject a secondary electron 
into the vacuum gap while scattering out itself.
Kinetic Outcome: Multi-lepton collision and ejection results in charge loss to 
the inter-electrode gap, penalizing eta_e.

Feynman Diagram:

  e⁻ (incident) \               / e⁻ (scattered / flies away)
                 \  γ* (virtual) \
                  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
                 /               \
  e⁻ (target)   /                 \ e⁻ (ejected secondary e⁻)


6. Lepton–Lepton Interaction: Thermalization (e⁻ ──γ*──> e⁻) [Gets Stuck]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Physical Process: Cascading soft electromagnetic collisions with conduction 
electrons (e⁻_1, e⁻_2, e⁻_3) thermalize the electron's kinetic energy into 
the collector's Fermi sea.
Kinetic Outcome: Momentum is randomized and energy is dissipated via plasmon/
electron-hole pair generation, trapping the electron as usable charge carrier.

Feynman Diagram (Microscopic Multi-Vertex Cascade View):

  e⁻ (incident) ───●───────────●───────────●───═══ e⁻ (stuck / Fermi sea)
                   │           │           │
                   │ γ*        │ γ*        │ γ*
                   │           │           │
                 e⁻_1        e⁻_2        e⁻_3  (conduction electron sea)
                   │           │           │
                   ▼           ▼           ▼
             [Plasmons / Electron-Hole Pair Generation → Thermalization]

III. KEY PHYSICAL INSIGHTS & REGIME ANALYSIS

1. Single Nucleus vs. Multiple Nuclei Absorption:
   • Single Nucleus (Intra-Nuclear Cascade): Occurs during initial impact where an 
     electron undergoes multiple soft scatterings (γ*) off bound quarks inside 
     the same heavy nucleus.
   • Multiple Nuclei (Inter-Nuclear Track): Occurs as the electron penetrates 
     the collector lattice, sequentially interacting with bound quarks across 
     adjacent nuclei along its stopping path until its kinetic energy is completely 
     dissipated into phonons (TC). Both channels operate simultaneously.

2. Single Interaction Absorption (1-Step Capture):
   • While capture is typically a multi-collision cascade, an electron CAN get 
     stuck in a single interaction if the energy transfer ΔE ≈ Ek drops its 
     remaining momentum below the material's surface work function in one step.

3. Initial Kinetic Energy Regimes:
   • Low Kinetic Energy (Ek ≈ few eV, thermal emission): Single-interaction or 
     few-step capture dominates because the electron carries minimal initial 
     momentum, making it lose its escape velocity almost instantaneously.
   • Higher Kinetic Energy (Ek >> few eV, accelerated potential): Multi-step 
     cascade dominance occurs because high-velocity electrons penetrate deeper, 
     requiring a chain of successive electromagnetic collisions across the 
     electron sea and nuclear lattice to fully thermalize.
